Widen for iPhone Duo

Fold your Duo and Safari gives up nearly a fifth of the screen. Widen gets it back.

On the outer display, Safari parks its buttons in an 84‑point column next to the camera and squeezes every page into what’s left. Widen is a small Safari extension that lays pages out across all 466 points instead, so articles, photos and tables run right to the edge, while the buttons float on top as before.

Your way

More than an on/off switch

Widen’s panel in Safari has a few settings for the times one rule for every site isn’t quite right.

Rules for each site
Set a site to Always or Never and Widen remembers, whatever your general setting. Keep your bank in its original layout and your favourite news site wide, for example.
Desktop sites, on request
For a site that only has a desktop layout, turn on widening for that site too. Widen sizes the page to fit the display, then stretches it by the same amount it would a phone layout.
Portrait, landscape or both
Some people only want the extra room when reading upright, others only on their side. Choose which orientations Widen works in.
Fewer pop-overs
An optional setting tucks away cookie notices and “sign up for our newsletter” banners, which take up a lot of a small screen. It’s off unless you turn it on.
Shortcuts actions
Set Widen and Toggle Widen work in the Shortcuts app, in automations and with Siri. You could have Widen come on when you fold the phone at bedtime, for instance, or put a toggle on the Action button.
Getting started

Set up in about a minute

  1. Install and open Widen

    The app walks you through it. Tap Open Safari Settings to jump to Widen’s page in Settings.

  2. Turn the extension on

    Switch on Allow Extension. Only you can do this: iOS doesn’t let any app switch on its own Safari extension.

  3. Let it reach websites

    Set All Websites to Allow. Widen needs that access to change a page’s width, and it sends nothing anywhere.

  4. Choose Widen in Safari

    Fold the phone, open any page, tap the menu button in the address bar and pick Widen. Tap the wide picture, and every site follows.

FAQ

Good questions

Is Widen any use without an iPhone Duo?

No. Only the Duo’s outer display has Safari’s side column of buttons, so that’s the only place Widen changes anything. On the inner display, and on other iPhones, it stays out of the way.

I switched it on but a site looks exactly the same. Why?

Usually one of three things. The site may use a desktop layout, which Widen skips unless you set that site to Always with desktop layouts included. The site may fix its own width, so there’s nothing to stretch. Or Safari hasn’t given Widen access to that site yet: check that All Websites is set to Allow in Widen’s extension settings, then reload the page.

Can I keep one site the way it was?

Yes. Open Widen’s panel on that site and set it to Never. You can also do the reverse: leave Widen off in general and set the few sites you read most to Always.

What happens after the seven days?

Unless you buy the unlock, pages go back to Safari’s normal layout. Your settings and site rules are kept, so if you buy later everything carries on as before.

Can Widen see what I’m browsing?

It runs on the page so it can change the layout, but it never records or sends anything. Widen makes no network connections of its own and has no analytics. The privacy policy has the details.

Can a shortcut turn it on and off?

Yes. Look for Set Widen (on or off) and Toggle Widen in the Shortcuts app. Open pages follow the new setting straight away.

How do I stop using it?

Choose Off in Widen’s panel in Safari, or in the app. To remove it altogether, turn off Allow Extension in Safari’s extension settings, or delete the app.

Something isn’t right. Who do I tell?

Send a note through the support page. Include the site’s address and which way up the phone was, and it can usually be fixed quickly.
